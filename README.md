# Brass Practice

A practice app for tuba and other brass: fingerings, scales, rhythm, real pieces and a tuner. It runs entirely in the browser with no server and no install, and works offline.

**To run it:** open `index.html` in Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari.

**Or use it online** at https://giituu44.github.io/brass-practice/ and install it: *Add to Home screen* (Android, or Share › Add to Home Screen on iPhone and iPad) or the install button in the address bar (Chrome, Edge). After one visit it keeps a copy of itself and all its sounds (about 9 MB), so it opens and plays with no internet. When a new version has downloaded, it says so at the top: **Reload** to switch to it. On iPhone and iPad the home-screen app keeps its own progress, apart from Safari's: move it across with Back up and ⬆ Restore (Settings).

## What's in it

- **Today**: a daily routine sized to the time you have (10, 20, 30 or 45 min), or **your own routine** (tick the steps you want). **▶ Start session** runs the steps one after another, with a short rest between, from a bar along the bottom. A **trouble spot** step brings back the bars you missed most in a piece (until you play them clean), a **review** step a piece you played well but not for two weeks. **Goals** (a piece cleanly at a tempo by a date, or minutes a week) fill in as you practise. A **Get set up** checklist covers the range, compensating valves, mic and delays. And a **journal** for the day: warm-up, scale of the day, note drill, rhythm and a piece, plus lip slurs and sight-reading at 30 min, and the relative scale and both a study and a piece at 45. The piece rotates among your next few unfinished ones. With minutes practised this week, a streak, and progress for the current instrument.
- **Warm-up**: the fundamentals in one place — including the play-along warm-ups (today's is marked). **Breathing** (a paced in/hold/out circle), **mouthpiece buzzing** (hear a siren or short line, buzz it, and see your pitch drawn over it), **long tones** with a dynamic shape, **tonguing** against the click, **lip slurs**, and a **range builder** that pushes your highest and lowest clean notes a step at a time and remembers your records.
- **Recordings**: play along with a real recording (MP3, M4A, WAV…). Drag across the waveform to loop a passage, slow it down without changing the pitch (optionally 5% faster each time round). Recordings, loops and speeds are kept in this browser.
- **Echo**: ear training by call and response. The app plays a short phrase (four levels), you play it back in the next bar by ear, on the valves or the mic; then the music is shown and graded.
- **Progress**: minutes per week over 16 weeks, personal records, tempo and score piece by piece, your tuning over time, recent journal notes, and a one-page **weekly report** to print or send to a teacher.
- **Fingerings**: name-the-fingering drills that give your weak notes (and notes you haven't seen for a few days) more often. You answer with Space, automatically, or against a metronome. Read in any key signature, or turn on ear training. Brass band players can read **treble clef as a brass band part** (B♭ and E♭ bass, euphonium, trombone), and euphonium and trombone players **tenor clef**.
- **Scales**: every major and minor scale plus chromatic, one or two octaves, written with key signatures; straight, in thirds, in groups of four (Clarke-style) or as an arpeggio. Play along with a metronome (each beat is graded) or go at your own pace. A loop option speeds up after each clean run.
- **Pieces**: the music scrolls past a gold play line (or shows as a page with a moving playhead). Your note sounds as you finger it, a wrong fingering sounds the note it would really make, and misses show the right fingering. Real notation with a built-in library (warm-ups, lip slurs, technical studies, études, melodies) and a sight-reading generator (five levels; 4 and 5 add wider leaps, sixteenths, triplets and notes outside the key). Play along graded, wait-for-me, or listen. Loop a passage, change the tempo, move it to any of the 12 keys (key signature and spelling follow), or shift the octave. The clef and key stay pinned at the left while the music scrolls, and after a run it points out the bars where most misses were, with a one-click slower loop of them. With the mic on, each run is recorded so you can hear your take back with the music scrolling along, and you get a tuning report (which notes sat sharp or flat, marked on the score). A miss tells you what your valves actually made ("you held 4 — that's C3"), and Loop can speed up 4 bpm after each clean run. Press B for the library browser (search, categories, your best scores).
  - **Duets**: you play one part and the app plays the other (a chorale, a round, Ode to Joy with a bass line). **Excerpts**: the Mahler 1 canon and the Dies irae Berlioz gives the tubas (written from the melodies; check them against a real part).
  - With the mic, a **pitch trace** is drawn through each note after a run (scoops, sags and late centring show at a glance), and every graded run's recording is **kept** under History (newest 12 per piece, plus your best) so you can hear how it has come along.
  - **Tempo coach**: after a whole run, a 🎯 button suggests the tempo to try next (faster after a clean run, slower after a rough one).
  - **Fingering hints** (Options): always, or fading as you learn — every note for your first two runs, then only the notes you missed last time.
  - **Backing** (Options) for music without its own: a drone on the key note, or simple chords worked out from the melody.
  - **Dynamics**: p, mf, f… marks are read from ABC and MusicXML and shown under the music; with the mic, it measures how much louder your loud marks were than your soft ones.
  - **Stand mode** (⛶ or F): big music, no menus, and the screen stays on — for a tablet or phone on the stand.
  - **📝 Notes**: your own notes on each piece, shown whenever you open it. If your intonation map found that an alternate fingering sits better on your horn, it's written under the notes that use it.
  - **Import** MusicXML or MXL (from MuseScore, Sibelius, Finale or Dorico) or ABC, or write ABC with a live preview.
  - **PDF**: a PDF exported from notation software (MuseScore, LilyPond, Sibelius, Finale, Dorico) has its notes **read automatically**: pitch, rhythm, key, clef (bass, treble, alto, tenor), accidentals, ties, triplets, and repeats with 1st and 2nd endings, played out in order. The result appears beside the page so you can check and fix it. On LilyPond PDFs scored against their MIDI files, it got 97–99% of pitches and about 95% of rhythms right, and MuseScore PDFs read correctly in spot checks. Scanned PDFs are only pictures, so for those you copy the notes in with a click-or-type note palette.
- **Method books**: all 60 of Kopprasch's *60 Studies, Op. 6* (the classic low-horn book tubists study), at concert pitch, from the Mutopia Project edition (CC BY-SA 3.0). Every note was checked against Mutopia's MIDI files. Plus 53 **Bordogni vocalises** (the "Rochut" melodious études, Rochut numbering and keys), from Michael Shirley's public-domain transcriptions of the original editions (github.com/muschem/trombone, CC0). Both books are set for tuba: bass clef, with the octave chosen for your instrument.
- **Piano accompaniment** for the Bordogni vocalises (from the original piano parts), played by a built-in electric-piano sound and following any key change; **breath marks** where Bordogni put them. **‹ ›** (or `[` `]`) steps through a book, and a clean run offers the next study. Today's routine alternates a piece with the next study from your books.
- **Metronome** (the metronome icon in the sidebar): tempo, beats per bar (accent groups like 3+2 for 5, 7 or 9), eighths/triplets/16ths, tap tempo, **silent gap bars** to test your inner pulse, and a gradual **speed-up**; it keeps going while you switch tabs, for practising from paper music.
- **Save your take**: after a mic run, ⬇ Save downloads the recording as an audio file.
- **Rhythm**: a metronome, and rhythm reading at five levels (up to ties, sixteenths and triplets) in 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 or 6/8, with millisecond-accurate scoring. Tap it, or with the mic on, play it on your instrument.
- **Tuner**: note, cents and fingering; an **intonation map** (play every note in your range, see which sit sharp or flat and roughly how many mm to move which slide); a **lip slur builder** (pick a fingering and pattern; it writes an exercise over that fingering's partials in your range, graded by the mic); a **long-tone challenge** with a dynamic shape (steady, crescendo, diminuendo or swell), graphed live, that shows whether your pitch moves as you get louder; a **tonguing trainer** (8ths, triplets or 16ths against the click: how even, how early or late, and whether your K syllables lag); and a drone. **Chord tuning**: the drone can play a root (or root and fifth) and the tuner aims for where an ensemble tunes your note against it — a major third about 14¢ low, a fifth 2¢ high. The tuner also shows what your intonation map says about the note you're on. The intonation map can also measure **alternate fingerings** (and tells you when one sits better on your horn) and keeps a **history**, so you can see your tuning improve.
- Built-in sounds are tuned to A = 440: each recorded sample was measured and is corrected at playback (the Iowa tuba recordings ran ~12¢ sharp on average). Pieces keep a history of every run (accuracy and tempo charts).
- **Microphone input**: play your real instrument instead of using the keyboard. It checks the right note and the right partial, and tells re-tongued notes on the same pitch apart.
- **Touch screens**: hold the pistons with your fingers like real valves (several at once).
- **Instruments**:
  - B♭ tuba, 4 or 3 valves
  - C, E♭ and F tuba
  - Euphonium
  - Tenor trombone (slide positions)
  - B♭ trumpet (treble clef, written pitch)

## Settings worth knowing

- **Valve keys** can be rebound, which helps if your keyboard can't register certain keys pressed together.
- **Timing** can be strict, normal or lenient.
- **Audio offset** corrects for the delay of Bluetooth headphones; **Calibrate** measures it by tapping along to clicks.
- **Mic sensitivity** (low for noisy rooms, high for quiet mics), a live **mic level** meter, and **Mic delay**, which you calibrate by playing short notes on the clicks.
- **Page-turner pedal**: → or Page Down acts as Space (start, check, next), ← or Page Up stops — so your hands can stay on the horn. **Hands-free start** (with the mic): play a short note and the count-in begins.
- **Count-in** of one or two bars (two leaves time to breathe); **rest reminders** after 15–30 minutes of playing without a break.
- **Keep the screen on** while the app is open (stand mode always does).
- **Compensating valves** (4-valve horns): the chart, tuner and intonation map say which fingerings run sharp on your kind of horn.
- **Colour-blind friendly colours** (blue / orange). Grading also shows marks (× wrong, › late), never colour alone; the app works from the keyboard (Tab to any control, Space or Enter to press it) with labels for screen readers.
- **Back up** keeps a copy of your progress; after a week of practice days without one, the app reminds you.
- **Tuning:** A = 438–446 Hz (e.g. 442 for many bands); sounds, drone, tuner and mic grading all follow it.
- **Theme:** auto, light or dark.
- **Sounds:** your note sound and the wrong-note sound can each be turned on or off.
- **Memorize** (in a piece's Options) hides the notes until you've played them.

## Keys

| | |
|---|---|
| `J` `K` `L` `;` | valves 1–4 (hold them like real valves; rebindable) |
| `1`–`7` | trombone slide positions |
| `Space` | check / start / tap |
| `H` | hear the note |
| `+` / `−` | tempo |
| `Esc` | stop |
| `?` | help |

## Files

- `index.html`, `style.css`
- `manifest.webmanifest`, `icons/`: the name, colours and icons for installing; `sw.js`: the service worker that keeps the offline copy (used only when the app is served over https or from localhost, not from disk); `js/pwa.js` starts it and offers new versions
- `fingerings.js`: the hand-made BB♭ tuba chart
- `js/`:
  - `core.js`: settings, the instrument, staff drawing, sound, valve input
  - `instruments.js`: chart generator for the other instruments
  - `pitch.js`: microphone pitch detection
  - `score.js`: ABC and MusicXML parsing and notation rendering
  - `omr.js`: reads notes from PDFs exported by notation software
  - `import.js`: the Add music dialog (files, PDF view, note palette)
  - `library.js`: the built-in pieces
  - `mode-*.js`: one file per tab
  - `pieces-gen.js`: sight-reading and lip-slur generators; `pieces-library.js`: the library browser; `takes.js`: kept takes and recordings (IndexedDB); `journal.js`: your notes; `session.js`: guided sessions; `goals.js`: goals; `stand.js`: stand mode and the screen wake lock
- `sounds/`: instrument samples as base64 MP3 in `.js` files, so they load even when the page is opened from your disk (only the set in use is loaded, when first needed)
- `tests.html`: open it to check note spelling, keys, the fingering charts, every built-in piece, the pitch detection and (through a local server) that every tab opens without errors
- `tools/check.ps1`: the one-click check — every script parses, `sw.js` lists every file the app loads, and the tests and app smoke test pass from disk in a headless Edge or Chrome (its own profile; your progress isn't touched): `powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File tools\check.ps1`. It also gives `sw.js` a new version whenever the files have changed, so installed copies update: publish `sw.js` along with them. (Served from localhost the app is cached just like online: after an edit, run the check, reload, and press Reload on the new-version notice.)
- `vendor/pdfjs/`: pdf.js 3.11, used to show and read PDFs (loaded only when you open one)
- `tools/`: the Python and ffmpeg scripts that built the sample files, `make-icons.ps1` (draws the PNG icons from `icons/*.svg`), and `mic-lab.html`: drop in a recording (e.g. a take saved with ⬇ Save) to see the pitch, note starts and tuning the app's mic detection reads from it
