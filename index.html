    Breathing

    Slow, full breaths before you play: follow the circle, in as it grows and out as it shrinks. One count a second.

     

    Mouthpiece buzzing

    Hear the line, then buzz it on the mouthpiece alone. Your pitch is drawn over it — the mic hears a buzz too.

     

    Long-tone challenge

    Hold one note as long and as steadily as you can, in the shape you pick. The timer starts when it hears the note.

    Tonguing

    Tongue one note in time with the click; see how even your notes are. For double tonguing (T-K), it shows whether the K notes lag. Use headphones.

    Lip slurs

    One fingering, moving between the notes it plays (its partials) in your range — air and lips only. The mic hears which partial you hit.

    Range builder

    Push your highest or lowest note one step further. A note counts once you hold it for a second, in tune. Rest between tries.

    Drone & chord tuning

    A steady note to tune against. Pick which note of the chord you're playing and the tuner aims for where an ensemble tunes it — a major third about 14¢ low, a fifth 2¢ high — not the tuner's equal temperament. Use headphones while the mic listens.

    About PDFs

    Choose a PDF above and it opens beside the note editor.

    • PDFs made by notation software (MuseScore, LilyPond, Sibelius, Finale, Dorico…): the notes are read automatically — pitches, rhythms, key, clef and accidentals. Pick which staff if there are several, check it against the page, and fix anything odd with the palette.
    • Scanned PDFs (photos of paper) hold only a picture, so copy the notes in with the palette as you read — or convert the scan with music-recognition software (MuseScore 4: File → Import PDF; or Audiveris) and import the MusicXML.

    Typing ABC directly

    C D E F G A B is the octave from middle C; c is an octave up, each , an octave down — so tuba notes look like B,, (B♭2 in B♭) or F,,, (F1). ^ sharp, _ flat, = natural. A number after a note multiplies the length set by L: (2 = twice, / = half, 3/2 = dotted). z is a rest, - ties to the next note, (3 starts a triplet, | is a barline, |: … :| repeats. Header lines: T: title, M: time signature, L: default note length, Q:1/4=90 tempo, K: key (last).

    How to use Brass Practice

    Your instrument (under the logo) sets the fingerings, range, clef and sounds; progress is kept per instrument.

    Answering

    Keys: hold J K L ; like real valves (or tap the pistons). Nothing held = open. Trombone: 1–7 for slide positions. Mic: switch ⌨ Keys / 🎤 Mic at the bottom of the sidebar to play your real instrument instead — it checks the note and the partial.

    Practice

    • Today — a routine sized to the time you have (10–45 min); Space starts the next step. Every other day the piece is the next study from your method books.
    • Warm-up — breathing, mouthpiece buzzing, long tones with a shape, tonguing, lip slurs and a range builder.
    • Echo — hear a short phrase, play it back by ear in the next bar.
    • Recordings — play along with a real recording: loop a passage and slow it down without changing the pitch.
    • Progress — your practice over months, records, tempo and tuning trends, and a weekly report for a teacher.
    • Fingerings — read a note, finger it. Auto moves on when you're right; Timed brings notes on the beat.
    • Scales — every major and minor, straight or in a pattern (thirds, groups of four, arpeggio), played along with the click (green on time, amber late, red wrong).
    • Pieces — warm-ups, études, melodies, sight-reading, and the method books: Kopprasch's 60 Studies and 53 Bordogni vocalises. B opens the library. The music scrolls past the gold line; you hear your note as you finger it, and misses show the right fingering. Under Options: start/end bars (or click a bar), loop (with a 4-bpm speed-up after clean runs), key, octave, guide, memorize. After a run it points out your trouble spot; with the mic it records you (P to hear your take) and shows which notes were sharp or flat. History charts your runs.
    • Rhythm — Space for a count-in, then tap each note (with the mic on, play them on your instrument). 2/4 to 6/8, up to triplets.
    • Tuner — tuner, an intonation map (play every note; see which sit sharp or flat and roughly how far to move which slide), a lip slur builder (pick a fingering; practise its partials with the mic), long tones with a dynamic shape, a tonguing trainer and a drone.
