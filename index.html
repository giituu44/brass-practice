    About PDFs

    Choose a PDF above and it opens beside the note editor.

    • PDFs made by notation software (MuseScore, LilyPond, Sibelius, Finale, Dorico…): the notes are read automatically — pitches, rhythms, key, clef and accidentals. Pick which staff if there are several, check it against the page, and fix anything odd with the palette.
    • Scanned PDFs (photos of paper) hold only a picture, so copy the notes in with the palette as you read — or convert the scan with music-recognition software (MuseScore 4: File → Import PDF; or Audiveris) and import the MusicXML.

    Typing ABC directly

    C D E F G A B is the octave from middle C; c is an octave up, each , an octave down — so tuba notes look like B,, (B♭2 in B♭) or F,,, (F1). ^ sharp, _ flat, = natural. A number after a note multiplies the length set by L: (2 = twice, / = half, 3/2 = dotted). z is a rest, - ties to the next note, (3 starts a triplet, | is a barline, |: … :| repeats. Header lines: T: title, M: time signature, L: default note length, Q:1/4=90 tempo, K: key (last).

    How to use Brass Practice

    Your instrument (under the logo) sets the fingerings, range, clef and sounds; progress is kept per instrument.

    Answering

    Keys: hold J K L ; like real valves (or tap the pistons). Nothing held = open. Trombone: 1–7 for slide positions. Mic: switch ⌨ Keys / 🎤 Mic at the bottom of the sidebar to play your real instrument instead — it checks the note and the partial.

    Practice

    • Today — a five-step routine; Space starts the next step. Every other day the last step is the next study from your method books.
    • Fingerings — read a note, finger it. Auto moves on when you're right; Timed brings notes on the beat.
    • Scales — every major and minor, played along with the click (green on time, amber late, red wrong).
    • Pieces — warm-ups, études, melodies, sight-reading, and the method books: Kopprasch's 60 Studies and 53 Bordogni vocalises. B opens the library. The music scrolls past the gold line; you hear your note as you finger it, and misses show the right fingering. Under Options: start/end bars (or click a bar), loop (with a 4-bpm speed-up after clean runs), key, octave, guide, memorize. After a run it points out your trouble spot; with the mic it records you (P to hear your take) and shows which notes were sharp or flat. History charts your runs.
    • Rhythm — Space for a count-in, then tap each note.
    • Tuner — tuner, an intonation map (play every note; see which sit sharp or flat and roughly how far to move which slide), a lip slur builder (pick a fingering; practise its partials with the mic), long tones and a drone.
